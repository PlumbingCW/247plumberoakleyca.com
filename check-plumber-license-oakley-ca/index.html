How to Check a Plumber's License in Oakley, CA

To check a plumber's license in Oakley, CA, search the contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov. It takes a few minutes and shows whether the license is active.

Call (866) 310-6010

By the Oakley 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated November 7, 2026

The California Contractors State License Board, known as CSLB, licenses plumbing contractors under the C-36 classification and keeps a free public record you can search by name, business or license number. You can also call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). A valid, active record is the first thing to confirm before anyone works on your pipes.

This guide shows what to read on the record, what to ask for in writing, which warning signs matter, and how permits and City business licensing fit in for homeowners in Oakley, CA.

What to look for on a CSLB record

Search the contractor and open the full record. These are the lines worth reading before you agree to anything.

Status is active

A license can be expired, suspended or cancelled. Only an active status means the contractor is currently allowed to take on work.

Classification includes C-36

C-36 is the plumbing contractor classification. A contractor licensed in another trade may not be qualified for plumbing work.

Business name matches

The name on the estimate, the truck and the contract should match the name on the record. A mismatch is worth asking about.

Bond and workers compensation

The record shows contractor bond status and whether workers compensation coverage is on file. Ask for current proof if the record is unclear.

Complaints and disciplinary notes

A record with open actions or repeated disciplinary entries deserves a closer look. One old entry is not the same as a pattern.

Three minutes at the keyboard

Go to cslb.ca.gov and open the License Check tool. Enter the license number if the contractor gave you one, or search by business name if not. Compare the name, the address and the status against what you were told.

If the contractor will not provide a number, treat that as a signal. A licensed professional has no reason to hide it. If you are hiring for a bigger project, such as a plumbing installation with new lines or fixtures, check the record before you accept any quote. A C-36 licensee is the classification that covers work under the California Plumbing Code, a state edition of the UPC.

Why the paperwork protects you after the job

Plumbing work hides behind drywall and under floors, so problems may show up weeks later. A licensed contractor with a written contract gives you a named business to go back to, and a public complaint process if the work falls short. With no license and no paper trail, you have little to point to.

Keep the contract, receipts, permit paperwork and photos of the finished work in one folder. They help with an insurance claim, a home sale or a warranty question on a fixture. If a defect turns up, written scope makes it easier to show what was promised and what was done.

The same habit applies to small jobs. A faucet swap and a sewer lateral repair both leave a record, and a quick CSLB search takes less time than the first phone call to an insurer.

Put the job in writing

A written contract should state the work, the materials, the price or how it is figured, and what happens if conditions change. It should name who pulls permits and who schedules any inspection. Ask for the contractor's license number to appear on it.

Be careful with a request for a large payment before any work begins. Pay as work progresses and keep copies of everything. For a water heater swap, the same rules apply, and our water heater installation page shows the kind of scope a proper job includes.

Red flags worth walking away from

Pushy door-to-door sales, a story that the price expires today and a demand for cash only should make you pause. So should a contractor who says no permit is ever needed. Permit rules vary by job, and the right answer is to ask the building department.

Check that the person standing at your door is the person on the record. Ask for photo identification if you are unsure. If something feels off, you can call the CSLB line to ask how to verify.

  • No license number offered or one that does not match the name
  • Unwillingness to put the scope and price in writing
  • A vehicle or flyer with no business name or contact details
  • Pressure to pay in full before work starts

Permits and business licenses in Oakley

The City of Oakley requires an active City business license to do business in the city, and permits can be issued to the owner, an authorized agent or a CSLB-licensed contractor. City Hall and the permit counter are at 3231 Main Street, and the Building Division can be reached at 925-625-7005.

The City's page does not list plumbing or water heater work specifically, so permit rules vary. Bring your job description to that counter, and have your contractor tell you in writing who will pull any permit.
