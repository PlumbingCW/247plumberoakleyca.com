Sewer Camera Inspection in Oakley, CA

Sewer camera inspection in Oakley, CA lets you see the inside of your lateral before you decide on a repair, a sale or a purchase.

It is a planned visit, scheduled when it suits you.

Call (855) 575-2890

A sewer camera inspection feeds a waterproof camera on a flexible rod through a cleanout and records the pipe from the house to the Ironhouse Sanitary District sewer. The video shows cracks, roots, offset joints, low spots and buildup, and a locator can mark the pipe's path and depth at the surface.

This page explains when a camera visit is worth booking, what the report does and does not show, and the district rules that make a CCTV inspection mandatory in some situations.

My toilets gurgle and there is a smell, but nothing has backed up yet

Gurgling, slow drains in more than one room or a faint sewer smell outside can appear weeks before a real backup. The cause could be a partial blockage, a root intrusion or a dip in the pipe that holds water.

The technical term for finding out which is a sewer camera inspection. It replaces guessing with a recorded view of the lateral, so you can act before the line stops.

If you live near Cypress Road, the Cypress Grove page explains how to tell a private lateral problem from a public main problem.

When the Ironhouse Sanitary District requires a CCTV inspection

The district requires the owner to have the lateral and main inspected by CCTV and to obtain a Compliance Certificate in several situations. These include the sale of a property more than 20 years old, a building permit of 25,000 dollars or more with no inspection in the prior 20 years, and a sanitary sewer overflow caused by the private line.

Unpermitted work discovered, a change of use, a subdivision, a qualifying sewer lateral service call, and replacement or significant repair of the lateral also count. Details are in District Ordinance No. 65, and the office at 925-625-2279 can tell you whether your situation qualifies.

If you are buying or selling, ask your agent early, because the inspection and certificate take time. The same video can serve as your own record of the pipe's condition. A camera report documents conditions against what the California Plumbing Code (the UPC family of codes), expects of a drain and sewer line.

What happens during a camera visit

We start at your cleanout, or at a removed toilet if there is none. The camera head, lit by LEDs, is pushed down the pipe while a monitor shows the live view, and the footage is recorded. The operator calls out features as they appear and notes the distance from the access point.

If the line has standing water or heavy buildup, the camera can only see so far. A drain cleaning or jetting pass first may be needed, and we will tell you if that applies. A locator at the surface can then trace the camera's transmitter to mark where the pipe runs and how deep it sits.

Afterward you receive the footage and a plain-language summary of what we saw and where. We do not decide for you. We explain what each finding means and what the options are.

What a useful inspection report should show

A recording alone is not much help without notes. Ask that the report identify these points.

Pipe material and diameter

Clay, cast iron, plastic and other materials fail in different ways, so identification shapes the advice.

Defects with distances

Cracks, offset joints and root entry should each be logged at a measured distance from the access point.

Low spots

A belly that holds water appears on camera as a pool in a section of pipe, and it explains repeat clogs.

Surface path and depth

The locator marks show where the pipe runs, which matters for any later digging or lining.

Limits of the survey

Any section the camera could not pass should be stated, so you know what remains unseen.

What the camera can and cannot tell you

A camera shows the interior of the pipe along the path it can travel. It does not measure soil conditions, test pressure or guarantee that a line will not fail next year. A clean video on one day is a snapshot.

Findings are reported factually, and any repair suggestion is a recommendation, never a requirement beyond the district's own rules. If damage appears, our sewer line repair page explains the options, including trenchless lining. If private sewer work is planned, the district requires plans by a California civil engineer, so we point that out early.

What affects the cost of a sewer camera inspection

A camera visit is priced by the work involved. You get a clear price before work starts, so the number is settled before the camera goes in.

  • Access to the line. A cleanout makes the visit quick. Pulling a toilet to get in takes longer.
  • Length of the lateral. Longer runs take more time to record and note.
  • Cleaning needed first. Heavy buildup or standing water may need clearing before the camera can see.
  • Locating and marking. Tracing the pipe's path and depth adds a step to the visit.
  • Report and footage. A written summary with distances and a copy of the recording takes extra time to prepare.
Insurance note

Insurers generally do not pay for inspections done for a sale or curiosity, but a camera recording can support a claim after sudden damage, so ask your insurer what documentation it wants. Keep the footage with your home records.
