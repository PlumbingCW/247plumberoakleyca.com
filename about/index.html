About Oakley 24/7 Plumbing

Oakley 24/7 Plumbing is a plumbing call line for homes and businesses in and near Oakley, CA, answered around the clock.

Call (855) 575-2890

This page explains what the service covers, how a call unfolds, what we leave to others, and how to confirm that any plumber you hire is licensed. It is written to set honest expectations before you pick up the phone.

What the service is

We repair and install plumbing: clogged drains, sewer lines, water heaters, hidden leaks, burst pipes, toilets, faucets and similar work. Urgent problems go through a 24/7 dispatch line, and planned jobs such as water heater installation or repiping are booked for a day that suits you.

Same-day appointments are offered when available, and a clear price comes before work starts. Our service area covers Oakley, Brentwood, Antioch, Knightsen and Bethel Island.

How a call works

A dispatcher answers, asks for your address and a plain description of what is happening, and checks whether anyone is in danger. If water is spreading, you are walked through closing the main valve and cutting power to wet areas while the visit is arranged.

A plumber then looks at the problem on site and explains what failed and what the options are. You receive the price before any work begins, and you decide whether to go ahead. If the scope changes once a wall or floor is opened, the work stops until you have heard the new number.

  • You describe the problem and give the address
  • Dispatch gives safe first steps for the wait
  • The plumber inspects and explains the options
  • You approve a clear price before work starts

What we do not do

We do not promise a set arrival time, because traffic, the job before yours and the schedule all move. The dispatcher will tell you honestly whether a visit is possible tonight or better as a same-day appointment when available.

We do not respond to gas leaks. If you smell gas, leave the building, call 911 and then PG&E at 1-800-743-5000 before you call us. We also do not decide permit rules, so ask the City of Oakley Building Division, and we do not judge whether your water is safe to drink, which is a question for your water supplier or a doctor.

How to verify any plumber's license

California licenses plumbing contractors through the Contractors State License Board, and the record is public. Go to cslb.ca.gov, open the License Check tool and search by business name or license number. Plumbing work falls under the C-36 classification.

Read the record for an active status, a matching business name and any open complaints or bond details. Run that check on any plumber who comes to your home, and ask for the license number and business name before work starts. Also ask for a written contract and the price in writing before work starts. A business working in Oakley also needs an active City business license.

The kinds of problems callers bring

Most calls fall into a few groups. Water heaters lead the list: a tank that leaks at the base, a pilot that will not hold, or a heater that has simply stopped making hot water. Our water heater replacement and repair pages explain how those jobs differ.

Drains and sewer lines come next, from a slow kitchen sink to every fixture backing up together, which usually means the main line. Hidden leaks, burst pipes and fixtures that will not shut off round out the rest, and the services index groups every page by problem so you can find yours quickly.

Planned work gets the same phone line. Installs, repipes and softener questions can wait for a normal appointment, and dispatch will tell you honestly whether yours is an emergency or something to schedule.

Where the service operates

Oakley sits in eastern Contra Costa County. Calls come from Oakley itself and from the neighboring communities of Brentwood, Antioch, Knightsen and Bethel Island, and the service area page lists the ZIP codes for each.

Knowing the area matters because the utilities change from town to town. Oakley water comes from Diablo Water District and its sewer from Ironhouse Sanitary District, so when you describe a leak or a backup we ask which side of the meter or the property line it sits on before we talk about repairs.

Honest limits

Some problems cannot be fully known until the pipe is open. A camera can show the inside of a sewer lateral, but it cannot see through a collapsed section, and a leak behind a wall may be larger than the stain suggests. When the picture changes, the price conversation changes with it.

This website gives general plumbing information, not an engineering opinion or legal advice. For your own house, the on-site visit is what counts.
