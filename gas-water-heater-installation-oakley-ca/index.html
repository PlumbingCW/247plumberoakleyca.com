Gas Water Heater Installation in Oakley, CA: What to Know

Installing a gas water heater in Oakley, CA joins gas, venting, water and earthquake bracing in one closet. For most homeowners the answer is to hire a licensed plumber and keep the gas side out of your own hands.

By the Oakley 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated November 7, 2026

A gas water heater installation is the job of removing the old tank, setting a new one, and connecting its gas line, flue, cold and hot water lines, temperature and pressure relief valve and seismic straps. In Oakley, CA that work happens in a home served by Diablo Water District for water and PG&E for gas, in Contra Costa County.

Some steps are within reach of a handy owner, but a mistake on the gas or vent side can put carbon monoxide or a gas leak inside the house. This guide walks through each connection, where homeowners get into trouble, and how to decide who should do the work.

Should a homeowner install a gas water heater at all?

Most people can lift the old tank out with a helper, and many can fit a new one into the same spot. The trouble is that a gas water heater is not an ordinary fixture. It burns fuel, sends exhaust up a flue, and holds dozens of gallons of hot water under pressure, so every connection has to be right before the first firing.

A swap that looks simple can hide a corroded gas shutoff, a flue pipe that no longer rises steadily, or a relief valve that dumps hot water onto the floor. A licensed plumber checks these as routine. Our water heater installation page explains how a new unit is sited, vented and connected if you want to see the full scope.

Six connections that decide whether the install is safe

Each item below is a place where a rushed or untested job causes trouble later.

Gas line and shutoff

A fresh connector or rigid pipe, a shutoff valve you can reach, and a sediment trap where the setup calls for one. Every joint is tested for leaks before the burner is lit, never judged by smell alone.

Flue and draft

The vent pipe has to rise steadily to the outdoors, with tight joints and the clearances printed in the manufacturer's manual. Poor draft pulls exhaust back into the room.

Combustion air

A gas burner needs fresh air to burn cleanly. A sealed closet or garage corner without enough openings can starve the flame and produce carbon monoxide.

Relief valve and discharge pipe

The temperature and pressure relief valve is the last safety device if the thermostat fails. Its discharge pipe should run downward to a safe spot, with no cap, plug or upward bend.

Expansion tank

On a closed water system, heated water has nowhere to expand and pressure climbs. A small expansion tank absorbs the swing and protects the tank, fixtures and supply lines.

Seismic strapping

California expects water heaters to be braced against earthquake movement. The local note below gives the law that applies.

If you smell gas, stop and leave first

Gas safety comes before any plumbing question. Gas odor near the heater means you walk out first, dial 911 from the yard or street, and then ring PG&E at 1-800-743-5000. Do not flip switches, light anything or hunt for the leak yourself. Call a plumber only after emergency crews say the house is safe.

If you close the gas valve yourself, only PG&E can restore service, so a shutoff is a pause and not a repair. A pilot that keeps going out, a lazy yellow flame, or soot around the draft hood are also reasons to stop and have the unit inspected, even with no odor.

What a proper swap looks like, start to finish

The order below is the general sequence. Your unit's manual controls the details, and any step that surprises you is a reason to pause.

  • Shut off the gas and the cold supply, then drain the old tank
  • Disconnect the old unit and note how the vent and gas were routed
  • Set the new tank level on a stable base and fit the strapping
  • Connect water lines, expansion tank and the relief valve discharge
  • Reconnect gas, leak test every joint, then light the burner by the manual
  • Check draft at the flue and confirm hot water at a faucet

What this means in Oakley

Our county is inside the Bay Area Air Quality Management District, which regulates the emissions of replacement gas tanks. Rule 9-6 has limited nitrogen oxide from gas water heaters for decades, and the zero-NOx standard for new residential gas units, once set for January 1, 2027, was delayed to January 2028 by amendments the Board adopted on November 4, 2026.

A Board vote on November 4, 2026 pushed that date back to January 2028 and added exemptions. Look up the date and any exemption that fits your home at baaqmd.gov before you commit to a gas model. The rule applies to new units at the time of replacement, not to the heater you already own.

State law covers strapping. Health and Safety Code 19211 says new and replacement water heaters, and existing residential ones, must be braced, anchored or strapped, and tanks of 120 gallons or less usually use a pre-engineered kit. We cannot promise either way whether a gas heater swap needs a City permit, so put that question to the Building Division staff by phone before the install date.

The permit counter is at 3231 Main Street, and either you or your contractor can apply. We cover Oakley and nearby towns, as the service area page shows. Neighbors in Brentwood live under the same Air District rules, though their permit questions go to the City of Brentwood Building Division.
