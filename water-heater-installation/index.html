This page is for scheduled jobs, so you have time to compare fuel types and ask the City about permits. If your current tank is already flooding, our replacement page covers the emergency route.

Sizing the unit to your household

Size comes from how many people shower in the same hour, how many bathrooms you have and whether a soaking tub or a long laundry run is part of daily life. A tank that is too small runs out of hot water, and one that is too big burns fuel keeping extra water warm.

We look at first-hour capacity for tanks and flow rate for tankless units, then match it to the space you actually have. Bring up any plan to add a bathroom, an in-law unit or a pool house, because that changes the answer.

Rules that shape an Oakley install

Fuel choice is partly a rules question. The Air District's zero-NOx date for residential gas water heaters moved from January 2027 to January 2028 under amendments adopted on November 4, 2026, and that date matters only for a unit being newly installed, never for one already in service.

Check baaqmd.gov for the exemptions that came with the later date before you order a gas unit.

Any new or replacement tank must be braced or strapped under Health and Safety Code 19211, using a strapping kit for standard units of 120 gallons or less. Before booking an install, ask the City of Oakley Building Division at 925-625-7005 if it needs a permit, and plan around the permit counter at 3231 Main Street being open Monday through Thursday.

What moves the price of a planned install

Because there is no emergency, you can compare options calmly. These are the pieces that decide the total.

  • Unit type and size. The unit itself leads the bill, and tankless and heat pump models run higher than basic tanks.
  • Moving or changing the location. Relocating a unit means new supply, drain and venting or electrical runs.
  • Fuel conversion. Gas to electric or the reverse needs a new circuit or gas line.
  • Code items. Seismic strapping, an expansion tank, a drain pan and a proper relief discharge line are standard parts of a clean install.
  • Permit and inspection. If the City requires a permit, plan for the time to obtain it. Ask the Building Division what applies.
Insurance note

A planned upgrade is normally an owner expense. Some insurers ask for proof of water-damage prevention features, so ask yours whether a pan, leak sensor or auto-shutoff earns anything.

Get ready for install day

A little preparation shortens the visit and avoids surprises.

Clear a path

Move stored items in the garage or closet so the old unit and new unit can pass without bumping into things.

Note the old unit's label

Photograph the rating plate showing fuel, capacity and model. We can plan parts from it.

Locate the shutoffs

Find the main water shutoff and, for gas, the gas shutoff. If you cannot locate either, tell us.

Plan for a water pause

Water to the whole house is off for part of the visit, so plan showers and laundry around it.

Ask about the future

If you might add a bathroom or solar, say so before we choose the unit.

Strapping, expansion and the small parts that matter

The installer's craft shows in the pieces nobody sees afterward. A closed plumbing system with a backflow device on the supply can trap pressure when the water heats, and an expansion tank gives it somewhere to go.

A relief valve with a full-size discharge line, a drain pan with a path to a safe spot and flexible connectors with isolation valves all make later service easier. If your home needs other fixture or line work at the same time, schedule it together so walls are opened once. We serve nearby Brentwood with the same approach.
