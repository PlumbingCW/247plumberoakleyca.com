Urgent

When to call right now

Call straight away if water is spreading across a floor, sewage is rising in a tub or toilet, or a water heater is leaking from the tank itself. Our 24/7 dispatch line stays open overnight, so use the steps below to protect the house while you wait.

Rotten-egg gas odor outranks every plumbing problem on this page, so step outside, call 911 and PG&E at 1-800-743-5000, and phone us afterward.

  1. Close the main water shutoff by turning it clockwise until it stops, then open one low faucet to bleed off pressure.
  2. Flip the breaker for any wet room where water is near outlets or appliances, and stay out of standing water.
  3. For a leaking water heater, close the cold-water valve above it and cut its power or gas control before anything else.
  4. Stop running every sink, tub and toilet if sewage is backing up, and keep children and pets away from the area.
  5. Lift rugs, electronics and boxes off wet floors, then photograph the damage for your insurer.
  6. Phone dispatch and say what you shut off, what you can see and whether anyone is in danger.

Problems an after-hours visit usually solves

Most midnight calls fall into a few groups. A tank that has split needs water heater replacement, while a unit that only lost its heat may need repair of an element, thermostat or valve. Water you cannot trace, like a damp wall or a meter that keeps turning, calls for leak detection before anyone cuts into drywall.

Pipes that split or crack get burst pipe repair once the water is off. A toilet that will not stop running can be handled through toilet repair, and a faucet or shutoff valve that will not close falls under faucet and sink repair. A jammed disposal under the kitchen sink is a smaller job, covered by garbage disposal repair.

A slow or blocked fixture starts with drain cleaning. When every drain in the house gurgles or backs up, the stoppage is usually in the main line, so the job moves to sewer line cleaning. Roots and grease that keep returning call for hydro jetting, and a cracked or collapsed lateral needs sewer line repair.

Who handles what: your pipes and the district's pipes

Diablo Water District answers for the line from the main to the meter box. Water pooling ahead of the meter is the district's to fix, anything past it is the owner's, and a quick call to the District settles which side you are on before you pay for any dig.

The street sewer belongs to the District, while the lateral that connects your house to it is yours. In October 2024 a public sewer main failed at East Cypress Road and Main Street, and the district's crews handled it. That is why the first question on any sewer call is whether the backup starts in your private line or in the street.

Planned jobs and business calls

Not every plumbing job is urgent. Tankless heater flushing, water heater installation, repiping, fixture and line installation and water softener and filtration can be booked for a day that suits you. A sewer camera inspection is also scheduled work, useful before a sale or after a repeat backup.

Businesses get the same after-hours line. A supply leak in a shop or a floor drain overflow in a restaurant kitchen is handled under commercial plumbing, and a floor that stays warm over a concrete slab can lead to slab leak repair. Work is expected to meet the California Plumbing Code, which the state builds on the UPC.

How pricing works before we start

You get a clear price before work starts, after the problem has been seen. The number depends on access, the pipe material, how far the failure runs, parts and whether a permit applies. Whether a job needs a permit depends on its scope, and the City decides that, not us.

Sudden and accidental water damage may be covered by homeowners insurance, while slow wear usually is not. Photograph everything, keep the failed part if you can, and ask your insurer before cleanup. The contact page lists what to have ready when you call.
