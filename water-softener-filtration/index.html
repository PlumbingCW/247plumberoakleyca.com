Water Softener and Filtration in Oakley, CA

Water softener and filtration service in Oakley, CA starts with the number on the water report: Diablo Water District reports an average hardness of 124 mg/L. This is a planned job, so there is time to choose carefully.

We install, repair and service softeners, conditioners and filters.

A water softener swaps the calcium and magnesium that cause hardness for sodium or potassium, while a filter removes particles or tastes without softening. They solve different problems, and many households want one, the other or both.

This page explains what the local numbers mean, compares the main options, covers where a softener goes, how repairs work and what shapes the price. We do not give health advice about water, and we say so where it matters.

What 124 mg/L means at an Oakley tap

Across the 2025 sampling, Diablo Water District measured hardness in a band from 87 up to 150 mg/L, centered on 124. That works out to about 7 grains per gallon, using 1 gpg as roughly 17.1 mg/L. On the commonly used scale, 61 to 120 is moderately hard and 121 to 180 is hard, so the average sits at the low end of hard.

In practice that means white scale on faucets and showerheads, spotted glassware and mineral build-up inside water heaters. Whether it is worth treating is your choice, and a homeowner should not feel pushed. Ask us to look at your fixtures and heater first, and we can show what scale is actually doing.

Softener drains and bypass valves are installed to the California Plumbing Code, whose parent is the UPC, and a plumber should explain how the brine line reaches the drain.

Softener, conditioner, filter or reverse osmosis

These products are often confused. The table separates what each one does, and it is honest about what each one does not do.

SystemWhat it doesWhat it does not do
Salt-based softenerRemoves hardness minerals with ion exchange, regenerates with saltDoes not filter taste, and adds sodium in proportion to hardness removed
Conditioner or descalerChanges how scale forms without removing mineralsDoes not lower hardness readings
Whole-home filterCatches sediment or chlorine taste at the mainDoes not soften water

Signs scale is costing you

You do not need a lab test to see hardness at work. These are the common signs in a home on Diablo Water District water.

White crust on fixtures

Faucet spouts, showerheads and sink edges collect crusty deposits.

Slow showerhead

Spray holes narrow as minerals build up.

Noisy or slow water heater

Mineral layers build at the bottom of the tank and on elements. See water heater repair for what that looks like.

Spotted dishes

Dishwasher cycles leave film on glass and cutlery.

Where a softener goes and how it is installed

A softener is normally placed after the main shutoff and before the water heater, with a drain nearby for regeneration water and an outlet for the control head. Outdoor hose bibs are often left on hard water to avoid wasting softened water on lawns.

We bypass the unit, plumb it in with shutoffs, set the hardness figure, program regeneration and test for leaks. Tell us your brand if you already bought one. If the project involves a larger remodel, our plumbing installation page covers the planning.

Sodium, salt and what we will not claim

Diablo Water District reports an average sodium level of 59 mg/L in its water. A salt-based softener adds sodium to the softened water, with more added when hardness is higher. That is a fact about the process, not a medical judgment.

We do not diagnose health effects of water or recommend a system for health reasons. If sodium matters in your household, ask your doctor, and ask us about a separate unfiltered tap or potassium-based salt.

Repairing a softener or conditioner

Common faults include a control valve that stalls, a clogged injector, a salt bridge in the tank, a broken brine line and damage after a pipe was cut during other work. We check the bypass, pressure, drain line and settings before replacing parts.

If you only need a valve replaced or a unit reinstalled after a move, tell us the model. Some older units are cheaper to replace than to repair, and we will tell you which side yours falls on.

What shapes the price

Prices change with the equipment and the plumbing around it, and we give a clear price before work starts.

  • System type. Softeners, filters and reverse osmosis each use different hardware.
  • Capacity. Household size and hardness determine unit size.
  • Plumbing changes. Bypass valves, new shutoffs and a drain connection add labor.
  • Location. Garage, utility closet or crawl space installs differ in access.
  • Repair versus replace. Valve, injector and control head repairs depend on the model.
Insurance note

Treatment equipment is a planned purchase and is not usually an insured loss. If a softener's drain or brine line leaks and damages floors suddenly, ask your insurer whether that damage is covered.
