Plumbing Installation in Oakley, CA

Plumbing installation in Oakley, CA means adding or relocating fixtures, supply lines and drains, from a new kitchen sink to a full remodel rough-in. Gas smell comes first on this page, because it changes who you call.

After that, we cover planning, permits and what affects the price.

Plumbing installation is the work of putting in new pipe, fittings and fixtures that were not there before, such as a shower, washer hookup, kitchen sink or a whole bathroom. It differs from repair because the layout, venting and drain slope are designed first.

Oakley's housing grew from 18,374 people in 1990 to 43,357 in 2020, so most of the housing dates from the 1980s onward. Before a remodel, ask a plumber to identify your supply pipe material. This page covers gas safety, how we plan an installation, which jobs need permits and how pricing works.

Urgent

Smell gas? Leave first, call second, plumber third

A gas odor is never a plumbing appointment. Get everyone out of the building before you do anything else, and do not stop to open windows or find the source.

  1. Leave the building now with everyone and pets, and leave the door open behind you.
  2. Do not flip switches, use a lighter, start a car in the garage or use a phone inside.
  3. From outside, call 911.
  4. Then call PG&E at 1-800-743-5000 to report the leak.
  5. Stay out until emergency crews or PG&E say it is safe.
  6. Only after that, call us for an inspection or repair. If you shut the gas off yourself, PG&E must restore it.

Gas appliance hookups and gas line work

Connecting a stove, dryer, grill or fireplace, and converting an appliance between natural gas and propane, begin with a conversation about the exact appliance and the run of pipe. Rusty or old gas lines are checked for corrosion and leaks, and any work is pressure tested afterward.

The City's website does not list plumbing permits specifically, so a phone call to the Building Division at 925-625-7005 is the way to learn whether gas work needs one. We also tell you when a job calls for the utility instead of us.

Permits, inspections and sewer connections in Oakley

Plumbing permit rules depend on the scope of work, so visit the permit counter at 3231 Main Street or phone ahead before you start. Contractors working in Oakley need an active City business license, and they usually pull the permit on your behalf.

Anything touching the sewer is separate. A new or extended sewer lateral is regulated by Ironhouse Sanitary District, so a drain run that reaches the street side needs the District involved and engineered drawings. A new bathroom whose drain ties into the lateral deserves a call to the district at (925) 625-2279 before any trench is cut.

Projects in nearby Brentwood answer to the City of Brentwood Building Division instead, and our service area page lists the towns we cover. Finished work should meet the California Plumbing Code, the UPC as adopted with California amendments, and an inspector can check it where a permit applies.

Sump and ejector pumps

If a basement, utility room or low bathroom sits below the sewer line, an ejector pump lifts waste up to the lateral, and a sump pump moves groundwater out. Ironhouse Sanitary District says a backflow device may be required when a pump lifts sewage to the lateral, so confirm that with the district before choosing or replacing one.

Washers, new showers and larger kitchen projects

Washer and dryer hookups need a supply with shutoffs, a standpipe or drain box and a vent that works with the drain. New showers need a valve in the wall, a proper drain and a waterproofed pan, which is why we coordinate with the tile installer.

For a replacement water heater, see water heater installation, and for a whole-house supply upgrade, repiping is the better fit. A fixture that is not draining well after installation usually points to a problem in the drain run.

What affects installation pricing

Installation is quoted by scope, and scope depends on what is behind the walls. Installation quotes are set out before tools come out.

  • Distance from existing lines. A fixture close to existing supply and drain pipes costs less than a new run across the house.
  • Access. Slab floors, finished walls and tight crawl spaces add time.
  • Fixture selection. You or we supply the fixtures, and quality varies a great deal.
  • Permits and inspection. Permit fees and inspection timing depend on the City and the job.
  • Repairs the project uncovers. Old pipe, failed valves and poor venting are common discoveries in remodels.
Insurance note

Installation is normally a planned improvement and not an insured loss, though a leak or flood caused by faulty work may be covered. Keep invoices, permits and photos of pipe before walls are closed.
