Check the builder's warranty before you book a repair

A new-construction home often carries a builder warranty on workmanship and a separate manufacturer warranty on the water heater and fixtures. The terms differ by builder and by year, so read your closing paperwork first. A repair done outside the warranty process can complicate a claim.

If the problem is an active leak, shut off the water first. Then call the builder's warranty line and a plumber on the same day, so nothing floods while the claim is opened.

Where small leaks tend to show up first

These are general places to look in any home. They are not a claim about what is happening in Emerson Ranch.

The water meter

Turn everything off and watch the low-flow dial. If it keeps turning, water is escaping somewhere. Our leak detection page explains how a hidden leak is traced.

Irrigation and hose bibs

A wet patch on a lawn strip or a spigot that drips after it is shut off is an outdoor leak, and it adds to the water bill quietly.

Toilet tanks

A flapper that does not seal lets water trickle into the bowl all day. A drop of food coloring in the tank shows it in minutes, and toilet repair covers the fix.

Under sinks

Supply lines and traps can weep at the connection. Look for damp cabinet floors and mineral crust on the fittings.

What the 20-year sewer rule means for newer homes

The sewer district's camera inspection rule applies only to older properties at the time of sale. Check the year built on your county record. A home under 20 years old may not be covered yet.

It will matter later, and the lateral is the owner's responsibility at any age. Ironhouse Sanitary District can confirm whether a sale triggers the inspection.
