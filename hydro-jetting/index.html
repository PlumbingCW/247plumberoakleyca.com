Emergency Hydro Jetting in Oakley, CA

Hydro jetting in Oakley, CA uses a pressurized water stream to scrub the inside of a drain or sewer pipe, not just punch a hole through the clog.

It suits lines that keep filling back up, once a camera shows the pipe is sound enough to take it.

Call (866) 310-6010

Hydro jetting is a cleaning method in which a hose with a rotating nozzle sends high-pressure water forward and backward along a pipe, stripping grease, scale, silt and fine roots from the walls. It works best on 3-inch to 6-inch lines, such as a kitchen branch, a laundry drain or the sewer lateral running to the Ironhouse Sanitary District main.

Below you will find the signs of a line that needs more than a cable, what the process looks like, when we decline to jet, and what shapes the price. Routine fixture clogs are handled by our drain cleaning service.

My drain keeps clogging again, even after it gets cleared

When a line opens up with a snake and then slows down again within weeks or months, the cable usually removed only the middle of the blockage. A film of grease, mineral scale or root hair stays on the pipe wall and catches the next batch of debris.

The technical name for the fix is hydro jetting. It cleans the full circle of the pipe instead of drilling a narrow hole, which is why it is the usual answer to a repeat clog in a main or a long branch.

Backed up today and a jet might be the answer

A sewage backup, a toilet that will not clear or a floor drain bubbling up is an emergency whatever the eventual method. Our dispatch is open 24/7, and we decide at the cleanout whether a cable can open the line first or whether the line needs jetting. While you wait, reduce what goes into the pipe.

  1. Stop using sinks, showers, the dishwasher and the washer until the line is clear.
  2. Do not flush a backed-up toilet again, since each flush pushes more water toward the lowest opening in the house.
  3. Find your cleanout cap outside, if you know where it is, and tell us whether it is accessible or buried.
  4. Keep children, pets and bare feet away from any floor where sewage has surfaced.
  5. Photograph affected areas for your records before towels and cleanup change the scene.

What happens inside the pipe during a jetting visit

A technician opens the cleanout and feeds a hose with a small nozzle into the line. Water exits the nozzle through jets angled backward, which pulls the hose forward and makes the nozzle spin. On the way back, the operator slows down over trouble spots and works the nozzle back and forth.

Different nozzles do different jobs. A penetrating nozzle drills through a hard plug, a rotating nozzle scours scale and grease off the walls, and a root-cutting nozzle shaves roots from joints. The flow carries debris toward the district sewer, and we usually run a camera afterward to confirm what is left.

Which lines are candidates for jetting

Jetting earns its cost where a cable can only do a partial job. These are the situations where we usually recommend it.

Kitchen lines packed with grease

Years of cooking fat form a layered coating that only moving water at pressure will remove completely.

Roots entering at joints

A root-cutting nozzle clears the hairs and thicker strands that a cable head tends to push aside.

Mineral scale on older pipe

Hard deposits narrow the opening over time, and a jet can restore much of the original diameter.

A main that clogs yearly

Cleaning the whole wall resets the clock, and many homeowners then book a periodic cleaning.

Jetting or cabling: how the two methods differ on the same pipe

Both methods are legitimate, and neither is better in every case. The right choice depends on what is coating the pipe and what shape the pipe is in.

When we say no to jetting, and what happens instead

Water at high pressure is not appropriate for every pipe. A line with a crack, a separated joint or a section that has dropped out of line can lose water into the soil, undermine the ground around it, or widen the damage. We will not jet a pipe that a camera shows to be failing.

If the camera finds that kind of damage, the conversation shifts to the repair options on our sewer line repair page, which include spot repair and trenchless lining. A clean, sound pipe is the starting point for lining, so jetting and repair often follow each other in sequence.

What goes into the price of hydro jetting

Jetting requires heavier equipment than a cable, so it is priced differently. You receive a clear price before work starts, based on what we can see and what you describe.

  • Length and diameter of the line. More pipe and a larger bore mean more time and more water.
  • Access point. A good cleanout speeds things up. Without one, we may need to pull a toilet or find another opening.
  • How heavy the buildup is. Light grease needs one pass. Layered scale or dense roots need several, with nozzle changes.
  • Camera work before and after. A look before confirms the pipe can take the pressure, and a look afterward shows the result.
  • Time of the call. Night, weekend and holiday dispatch carries different labor costs than a daytime appointment.
Insurance note

Homeowner policies often do not pay to clean a line that has built up slowly, since that is treated as maintenance. Sudden damage from a backup may be covered under some policies, so ask your insurer and keep photos and any camera footage to support a claim.

The Oakley lateral: who owns the line you are jetting

A jetted lateral is the owner's line, and the District's responsibility begins at the public main the lateral joins. If it overflows, the cleanup and any damage are yours to handle. Jetting that lateral is therefore a decision you make, and a camera recording of the finished job is a useful record to keep.

Jetting does not change what the state plumbing code (the California Plumbing Code, drawn from the UPC), requires of a sound drain line, which is why a camera check comes first.
