Water heater replacement means removing a failed tank (or an unrepairable tankless unit) and setting a new one in its place, connected to your water, gas or power, venting and drain. In Oakley, CA that usually happens in a garage, utility closet or side yard, and it often starts with a puddle at the base.

This page covers what to do first, how to tell a dead tank from a fixable one, which type to choose, the local rules that touch a swap in Contra Costa County, and what drives the final price.

Urgent

Tank leaking or flooding right now

A tank that leaks from the base or seams is finished, and the water will keep coming until the supply is closed. Our 24/7 emergency dispatch takes these calls at any hour, and the steps below limit the damage while you wait.

Water that reaches drywall, flooring or a stored box in a garage gets worse by the hour, so act before you phone anyone.

  1. Close the cold water shutoff on the pipe above the heater by turning the handle until it stops.
  2. Turn the power off at the breaker for an electric unit, or set a gas unit's control to off or pilot.
  3. If the closet or garage smells of gas while the tank is failing, walk away from it and call 911 and PG&E at 1-800-743-5000 before dispatch.
  4. Open one hot faucet so the lines can vent, and attach a hose to the drain valve only if the water is not scalding.
  5. Move boxes, tools and electronics out of the wet area, and photograph the water and the label for your insurer.

Signs the tank is past saving

Not every problem means a new heater. These are the signs that point toward replacement rather than a repair bill that will not last.

Water at the tank body

Moisture seeping from the shell or the bottom seam means the steel liner has let go. No valve or fitting swap fixes that.

Rust-colored hot water

Orange or brown hot water with clear cold water suggests the inside of the tank is corroding.

Rumbling and popping

Sediment baked onto the bottom makes the tank knock as it heats and wastes fuel. Diablo Water District reports an average hardness of 124 mg/L, so mineral buildup is a normal outcome of years of heating.

Repeated repairs

When a second or third part fails in a short stretch, the tank is usually wearing out as a whole.

Age of ten years or more

Check the date code on the rating label. A tank well past a decade that has stopped heating is rarely worth a new element or gas valve.

Local rules that touch a swap in Oakley

Gas tanks in the Bay Area fall under Air District rules. Your current heater is not affected by the Air District's zero-NOx rule, which targets the replacement unit and was moved from January 2027 to January 2028 on November 4, 2026.

The same amendments add exemptions, so read the rule text at baaqmd.gov before you buy a gas unit.

Every replacement and new tank also has to be braced or strapped against earthquake movement under Health and Safety Code 19211, which is why the strapping kit is part of the job. The City sets permit scope for swaps, so call the Building Division at 925-625-7005 and describe the old and new unit.

What sets the price of a replacement

Two houses with the same tank size can get very different totals. The price depends on what the old setup needs before the new unit can go in.

  • Type and capacity. A larger tank, a tankless unit or a heat pump each cost more than a basic tank of standard size, and the unit itself is the biggest line.
  • Fuel and connections. Changing from gas to electric, or the reverse, adds gas line, venting or circuit work.
  • Venting and code updates. Worn flue pipe, missing drain pans, a lacking expansion tank or an unsafe shutoff may need to be brought up to the California Plumbing Code.
  • Access and removal. Attic placement, tight closets and hauling the old tank away change the labor.
  • Water damage on arrival. Corroded shutoffs or leaking supply lines found when the old unit comes out add repair time. For flooded floors, ask about our leak detection service.
Insurance note

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a tank bursting overnight, is often treated differently by insurers than gradual wear or a slow leak. Ask your insurer before work starts, and keep photos of the tank, the water and the damage.

How a same-day swap goes

We start by confirming the failure and the fuel, then drain and remove the old tank. The new unit is set on a pan where space allows, connected with fresh flex lines and shutoffs, and strapped to the wall framing.

Gas units get a vent and leak check before lighting, and electric units are tested at the breaker. We run hot water at the farthest tap to clear air, then show you the shutoff, the temperature setting and the relief valve discharge line.
